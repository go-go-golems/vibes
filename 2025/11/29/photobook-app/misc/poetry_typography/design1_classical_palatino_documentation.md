# Design 1: Classical Palatino with Van de Graaf Canon

This design embodies a classical and elegant approach to poetry typography, drawing inspiration from the rich history of book printing. The primary goal is to create a reading experience that is both beautiful and timeless, with a strong emphasis on readability and harmonious proportions.

## Typographic Approach

The core of this design is the use of the **Van de Graaf canon** for page construction. This historical method, rediscovered and popularized by typographer Jan Tschichold, provides a system for creating aesthetically pleasing and functional margins. The resulting proportions of 2:3:4:6 (inner:top:outer:bottom) create a sense of balance and guide the reader's eye naturally across the page. The text block itself mirrors the proportions of the page, creating a sense of unity and coherence.

## Font Selection

The chosen typeface is **Palatino**, a classic serif font designed by Hermann Zapf. Palatino is renowned for its elegance, readability, and versatility, making it an ideal choice for a traditional poetry layout. Its calligraphic influences and open counters contribute to a warm and inviting feel, while its strong serifs ensure clarity and legibility, even in smaller sizes.

## Whitespace and Layout

Generous whitespace is a key element of this design. The ample margins, combined with a line spacing of 1.3, create a sense of openness and prevent the page from feeling crowded. This allows the poem to breathe and gives the reader space to pause and reflect. The layout is simple and symmetrical, with the poem centered on the page, further enhancing the sense of classical order and balance.

## Sources and Inspiration

- **Van de Graaf Canon:** The principles of this canon were researched from various sources, including the Wikipedia article on "Canons of page construction." [1]
- **Palatino Typeface:** The choice of Palatino was influenced by its historical use in fine book printing and its reputation as a highly readable and elegant typeface for literary works. [2]
- **Classical Book Design:** The overall aesthetic is inspired by the principles of classical book design, which prioritize harmony, proportion, and readability.
