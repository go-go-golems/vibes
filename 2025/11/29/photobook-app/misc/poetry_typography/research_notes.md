# Poetry Typography Research Notes

## Source 1: Reedsy - How Creative Poetry Book Layouts Can Elevate Your Verse
URL: https://reedsy.com/blog/poetry-book-layouts/

### Key Principles:

**Simplicity and Space**
- Simplicity is key - allow words to shine without constraints
- Clean, minimal layouts with very little in the way
- Generous whitespace creates space for the poet's ideas to breathe
- Headings can often be omitted in poetry (unlike fiction)
- The design should look effortless and natural

**Design Philosophy**
- "A poetry book's design is a graphic re-interpretation of the poetry collection, a reflection of its essence through a different artistic medium" - David Provolo
- The right layout design only takes liberties afforded by the text
- Aesthetic and composition are directed by the nature of the poems (alignment, line lengths, meter) and poet's vision
- Design should embody tone, mood, and atmosphere without distracting from content

**Typography Hierarchy**
- Focus on "unity of type" - simplicity and clarity
- Typeface should suit the spirit of the work
- Visual playfulness when appropriate

**Collaborative Process**
- Designer Sebastián Cudicio asks three fundamental questions:
  1. Whether the collection consists of text only
  2. Whether it requires a specific graphic solution or treatment
  3. Whether the book will include illustrations or images
- Layout designer considers mood and subject matter of poems
- Visual choices guided by or aligned with the poetry's themes

**Design Elements**
- Title page, introduction, table of contents
- Images and illustrations arranged around poems
- Solutions for page breaks when poems span multiple pages
- Front matter can get creative treatment

### Examples Mentioned:
- Designer Natalia Junqueira: Used blue color integral to author's concept (childhood associations)
- Thomas Henry's "Embers on a Pyre" designed by Sebastián Cudicio: Simple, serious layout aligned with dark themes

## Fonts to Explore:

- Garamond
- Goudy
- Times New Roman (though less elegant)

## Source 2: Wikipedia - Canons of Page Construction
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canons_of_page_construction

### Van de Graaf Canon
- Historical reconstruction of medieval manuscript and incunabula page division method
- Also known as the "secret canon"
- Works for any page width:height ratio
- Creates margins of 1/9 and 2/9 of page size
- Inner margin is half of outer margin
- Margin proportions: 2:3:4:6 (inner:top:outer:bottom) for 2:3 page ratio
- More generally: 1:R:2:2R for page proportion 1:R
- Type area and page size have same proportions
- Height of type area equals page width
- Popularized by Jan Tschichold in "The Form of the Book"

### Historical Context
- Canons are historical reconstructions based on measurement of extant books
- Used in Medieval and Renaissance-era book design
- Notion popularized by Jan Tschichold in mid-late 20th century
- Based on work of J.A. van de Graaf, Raúl Rosarivo, Hans Kayser
- Tschichold: "methods and rules upon which it is impossible to improve have been developed for centuries"

### Key Principle
- Divides page into pleasing proportions
- Creates functional and aesthetic margins
- Influenced modern book design and page layout principles


## Source 3: LaTeX Poetry Typography Resources

### LaTeX Packages for Poetry:
- **verse package**: Standard for typesetting verses, overwrites LaTeX's original verse environment
- **poemscol**: Commands for setting collections of poetry
- **poetry package**: Macros for typesetting poetry consistently
- **poetrytex**: Additional poetry typesetting tools
- **memoir class**: Specifically designed for typesetting poetry, fiction, non-fiction; supports base font sizes 9-60pt

### Recommended Fonts for Elegant Typography:
- **Palatino** (mathpazo package): Classic elegant choice, old but not strange appearance
- **Linux Libertine** (libertine package): Modern elegant serif
- **Garamond**: Classic French Renaissance typeface
- **Crimson**: With math support, elegant alternative
- **Charter**: Clean and readable
- **Utopia** (Adobe): Elegant serif option
- **Kpfonts**: Palatino-like alternative
- **STIX**: Modern elegant option

### Typography Principles from Research:
- Palatino is particularly favored for its elegant, classical appearance
- Tufte-LaTeX classes provide excellent examples of elegant typography with generous margins
- Focus on readability and classical proportions

## Source 4: Photobook Design Principles (Medium - partial access)
URL: https://medium.com/full-frame/designing-a-photobook-principles-and-layouts-1ad2cc0df5b4

### Key Principles:
- Designer's work should be invisible - content takes center stage
- Good edit/selection is crucial
- Three-act structure: introduction, main content, resolution
- Alternate between wide and detailed views to avoid monotony
- Measure dimensions, observe typography carefully
- Paper quality and feel matter

## Design Approaches to Explore:

### 1. Classical/Traditional (Van de Graaf Canon)
- 2:3:4:6 margin ratios
- Palatino or Garamond font
- Generous whitespace
- Traditional centered layout

### 2. Minimalist/Contemporary
- Extreme whitespace
- Modern serif (Crimson, Libertine)
- Asymmetric layouts
- Clean, uncluttered

### 3. Elegant/Refined (Palatino-focused)
- As user requested - Palatino typeface
- Classical proportions
- Generous margins
- Sophisticated spacing

### 4. Grid-based/Structured
- Strong geometric grid
- Consistent rhythm
- Mathematical precision
- Modern approach to classical canons

### 5. Expressive/Artistic
- Variable spacing reflecting poem rhythm
- Creative use of whitespace
- Typography as visual interpretation
- More experimental while maintaining elegance
